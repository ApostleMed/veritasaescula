GED → ALMA Bridge

Unique to VAI

Your GED is a starting line, not a dead end. The GED → ALMA Bridge closes the exact gaps between the GED and A Level, then you join ALMA.

The GED tests broad reasoning; A Levels assume subject-by-subject depth. The gaps are specific: algebra and trigonometry in Maths, moles and bonding in Chemistry, and precise terminology in Biology, plus the move from computer-based multiple choice to handwritten exam papers. A diagnostic finds your gaps, so your bridge lasts 6, 9 or 12 months, not a fixed two years.

  1. Diagnostic and interview. Non-calculator algebra, a mole calculation, short-answer biology and a written explanation.
  2. The Bridge, 6–12 months. Only the topics you are missing, taught live online.
  3. ALMA BC+M, 18 months. Six months with no exams to build both sciences, then Biology and Chemistry together, then Maths last.

About 24–30 months from the start of the bridge to your final A Level exams, ending with one combined Pearson Edexcel certificate.

ALMA – A Levels for Medical Aspirants

What is ALMA at Veritas Aescula Institute?

ALMA (A Levels for Medical Aspirants) is an 18-month, fully online, fast-track programme in Pearson Edexcel International A Level Biology, Chemistry and Mathematics for students who want to study medicine. The first cohort runs from January 2027 to June 2028.

Is ALMA the full A Level, or a shortened version?

It is the full qualification. Students study every unit of the Pearson Edexcel International Advanced Level (IAL) specification in all three subjects and sit the same official Pearson exams as IAL candidates worldwide. ALMA compresses the calendar, not the content.

How can A Levels be completed in 18 months without losing rigour?

Pearson's modular exams run in January, June and October, so ALMA spreads its exams across the June 2027, October 2027 and June 2028 series instead of waiting for one final sitting. Teaching is continuous and intensive, with live classes, weekly exam-style practice, timed tests and mock exams. Every unit is taught in full.

What are the three ALMA formats?

ALMA Regular studies all three subjects in parallel and sits two units of each in every series. ALMA BCM takes one subject at a time: Biology in June 2027, Chemistry in October 2027 and Mathematics in June 2028. ALMA BC+M has no exams in June 2027, sits Biology and Chemistry together in October 2027, and Mathematics in June 2028. All three finish in June 2028.

Which subjects and units does ALMA cover?

Biology (6 units), Chemistry (6 units) and Mathematics (Pure Mathematics 1–4, Statistics 1 and Mechanics 1). In each subject, the first three units form the IAS stage and the next three the IA2 stage.

Why Biology, Chemistry and Mathematics, and not Physics?

Biology and Chemistry are the subjects most medical schools in the EU and China assess. Mathematics builds quantitative skills and is part of entrance tests such as the CSCA and IMAT. Choosing Mathematics instead of Physics also reduces lab-based practical sittings for online study. Physics needed for the CSCA and IMAT is covered in our entrance-exam preparation classes.

Where do ALMA students sit their Pearson exams?

At the British Council exam centre in Myanmar, an official Pearson Edexcel centre. Registration for the 2027 exam sessions opens with the British Council in January 2027, and we guide students through it. Students in Thailand sit their exams at the Pearson centre in Bangkok.

If I sit exams in different series, will I get several certificates?

No. Your results are linked by one Unique Candidate Identifier. You request the cash-in in the June 2028 series, so Biology, Chemistry and Mathematics appear together on one combined Pearson Edexcel certificate, subject to Pearson Edexcel's certification requirements.

How are science practicals assessed in an online programme?

In Pearson Edexcel International A Level Biology and Chemistry, practical skills are assessed through written papers (Units 3 and 6) that test experimental design, data analysis, error analysis and evaluation. ALMA teaches these through demonstrations and virtual labs, and there is no separate laboratory exam.

When do ALMA students apply to medical school?

Applications for September 2028 intakes open around January 2028, and for Spring 2029 intakes around June 2028. Students apply using results from their 2027 exam series. Deadlines vary by university, so we plan each student's timeline individually.

Which medical schools does ALMA prepare students for?

ALMA is designed for English-taught medical programmes in the EU, such as in Italy, Hungary, the Czech Republic and Poland, and for English-taught MBBS programmes in China. Requirements differ by university, so our admissions team checks each student's targets.

Does ALMA prepare students for medical entrance exams?

Yes. From July 2027, after-school classes prepare students for the CSCA (China), the IMAT (Italy) and EU university entrance exams, alongside interview and Multiple Mini Interview (MMI) practice.

Who can join ALMA directly?

O Level finishers and Grade 11 finishers from the government system can join ALMA directly (Track A). All applicants take the Diagnostic Placement Exam and a Structured Admissions Interview.

I have finished Grade 10 or the GED. Can I join ALMA?

Yes, through Track B. If you pass the Diagnostic Placement Exam and interview, you join ALMA directly. If not, you first complete a 6–12-month bridge sized by the diagnostic. GED holders take the GED → ALMA Bridge and then join ALMA in the BC+M format.

What is the GED → ALMA Bridge?

A bridge programme, unique to VAI, that closes the exact gaps between the GED and A Level: algebra and trigonometry, quantitative chemistry, precise biology and A Level exam technique. It lasts 6, 9 or 12 months depending on the diagnostic. Students then join ALMA in the BC+M format, so the whole route takes about 24–30 months.

Why do GED students need a bridge?

The GED tests broad reasoning through computer-based, mostly multiple-choice questions and one combined science test. A Levels assume subject-by-subject depth and handwritten exam papers with full working. The bridge closes that gap so students can keep up with ALMA from the first day.

What is the Diagnostic Placement Exam?

A placement test with non-calculator algebra, a chemistry calculation, short-answer biology and a written explanation, taken with a Structured Admissions Interview. It shows whether a student is ready for ALMA or needs a bridge first, and how long that bridge should be.

Is ALMA fully online?

Yes. All teaching is live online with subject specialists, so students can study from anywhere in Southeast Asia and beyond. Exams are taken in person, at the British Council exam centre in Myanmar or the Pearson centre in Bangkok.

Are Pearson Edexcel International A Levels recognised by universities?

Pearson Edexcel International A Levels are accepted by universities around the world. Entry requirements differ by university and programme, and most medical schools also set their own entrance exam or interview, so students should check each university's requirements. Our admissions team helps with this.

How do I apply for ALMA?

Register for the Diagnostic Placement Exam and Structured Admissions Interview through our pre-registration form, or contact our admissions team. Places in the January 2027 cohort are limited.
